How to manage change orders in Jira

A change order is a written, approved change to the scope, price or timeline of a project. Here is what a good one contains and how to run the process in Jira.

What a change order should contain

FieldWhy it matters
What changesA short description the client understands, not a ticket key.
WhyThe client request or discovery that triggered it.
Budget impactThe amount and currency the client agrees to pay.
Timeline impactHow many days the delivery date moves, if any.
Affected workThe Jira issues concerned, so delivery can trace it.
Internal approvalWho on your side agreed, and when.
Client approvalWho signed on the client side, when, and any note.

The process, step by step

  1. Detect. Someone on the team spots a request that is not in the agreed scope.
  2. Draft. Write the change order from the Jira issue, with cost and timeline impact.
  3. Approve internally. The project lead checks the price and capacity.
  4. Send to the client. The client approves or rejects, with a reason.
  5. Record and invoice. Keep the signed document and add it to the next invoice.

Doing it in Jira

With Jira alone, you can model change orders as their own issue type with custom fields and an approval workflow. The limits are the client step (clients rarely have Jira accounts) and the document (Jira does not produce a client-ready change order).

Scope Change Orders adds the missing parts to Jira Cloud:

  • a change request created from any issue, with budget and timeline impact;
  • internal approval limited to project admins by default;
  • a client approval link that needs no Atlassian account and expires after 7 days;
  • a printable change order with the full approval trail, where every timestamp names its timezone;
  • a dashboard of recovered, pending and absorbed revenue.
